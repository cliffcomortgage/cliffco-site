Mortgage Glossary

Plain-English mortgage terms.

Definitions for the terms you'll encounter during the mortgage process, written for borrowers, not bankers. We've focused on the language around non-QM, DSCR, and reverse mortgages, since that's where most glossaries fall short.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ#

A

Adjustable-Rate Mortgage (ARM)
A mortgage with an interest rate that stays fixed for an initial period, then adjusts periodically based on a market index. A 5/1 ARM has a fixed rate for five years, then adjusts annually. ARMs can make sense when you plan to sell or refinance before the adjustment period begins, or when the spread between fixed and adjustable rates is wide enough to justify the risk. They're also available as non-QM products for borrowers who don't qualify under conventional guidelines.
Asset Depletion
An income qualification method used in non-QM lending where verified assets are converted into a hypothetical monthly income figure. A lender takes your documented balance (say, $1,200,000 in investment accounts), divides it over the loan term (360 months for a 30-year loan), and arrives at a qualifying monthly income of $3,333. No actual withdrawals are required. This program is designed for retirees, high-net-worth borrowers, and business owners who have significant assets but limited reportable income on paper.

B

Bank Statement Loan
A non-QM mortgage that qualifies borrowers using 12 or 24 months of personal or business bank statement deposits rather than tax returns or W-2s. Lenders analyze average monthly deposits to establish a qualifying income figure. This is particularly useful for self-employed borrowers whose tax returns understate actual cash flow due to legitimate business deductions. Tax-optimized returns are designed to minimize taxable income, not maximize borrowing power.
Business Bank Statement Loan
A variation of the bank statement loan that uses business deposits rather than personal ones. A business expense ratio of typically 50% is applied to gross deposits to estimate net income. If your business deposits average $20,000 per month, the lender uses $10,000 as your qualifying income. This works best for LLCs, S-Corps, and sole proprietors with consistent, well-documented business banking activity.

C

Cash-Out Refinance
A refinance in which you replace your existing mortgage with a larger loan and receive the difference in cash at closing. If your home is worth $600,000 and you owe $250,000, you might refinance into a $400,000 loan and walk away with $150,000. That money can be used for renovations, debt consolidation, investment, or anything else. Cash-out refinances are available through conventional, FHA, VA, and non-QM programs, including for self-employed borrowers who couldn't qualify with tax returns alone.
Closing Costs
Fees and expenses paid at closing, separate from the down payment. These typically include origination fees, appraisal, title insurance, government recording fees, prepaid interest, and homeowners insurance. Closing costs generally run 2% to 5% of the loan amount. In many programs, they can be rolled into the loan, paid by the seller through a negotiated credit, or, in down payment assistance scenarios, covered by a secondary program.
Conforming Loan
A conventional mortgage that meets the loan limits and guidelines set by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. In 2025, the standard conforming limit for single-family homes is $806,500. Loans that fall within this limit can be sold to the secondary market, which is why conforming loans generally carry the lowest rates. Loans above the limit are called high-balance or jumbo loans and follow different rules.

D

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
A ratio that measures whether an investment property's income covers its debt obligations. DSCR = Gross Monthly Rent divided by Monthly PITIA. A DSCR of 1.25 means the property earns $1.25 for every $1.00 in debt, indicating positive cash flow. A DSCR of 1.0 means it breaks even. Most lenders require a minimum of 1.0 to 1.25 to qualify. Cliffco offers programs that go as low as 0.75 with compensating factors like a larger down payment or strong reserves. DSCR loans qualify based entirely on property income, with no personal tax returns or W-2s required.
Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)
Your total monthly debt obligations divided by your gross monthly income, expressed as a percentage. If you earn $10,000 per month and carry $4,000 in monthly debt payments (including the proposed mortgage), your DTI is 40%. Conventional loans typically cap DTI at 43% to 45%. Many non-QM programs allow DTI up to 50%. DSCR loans bypass DTI entirely. The property's cash flow qualifies the loan, not the borrower's personal income.
Down Payment
The portion of the purchase price a buyer pays in cash at closing; the lender covers the rest through the mortgage. Primary residence conventional loans allow as little as 3% down. FHA requires 3.5%. Investment properties typically require 15% to 25%. A larger down payment lowers the loan amount, often eliminates mortgage insurance, and can unlock better rates. On non-QM loans, a higher down payment can also compensate for a lower DSCR or a credit score at the lower end of the range.
Down Payment Assistance (DPA)
Programs that provide funds to help homebuyers cover their down payment and sometimes closing costs. DPA comes in several forms: grants (never repaid), forgivable loans (forgiven after living in the home for a set number of years), deferred loans (due when you sell or refinance), and low-interest second mortgages. There are more than 2,400 active DPA programs nationwide. Cliffco is an approved lender for several programs in New York and New Jersey, including SONYMA.

E

Equity
The portion of your home's value that you own, calculated as market value minus the outstanding mortgage balance. A $500,000 home with a $300,000 mortgage has $200,000 in equity. Equity builds through principal paydown and property appreciation. It can be accessed through a cash-out refinance, home equity loan, HELOC, or, for homeowners 62 and older, a reverse mortgage. For real estate investors, equity is what funds the next acquisition.

F

FHA Loan
A government-insured mortgage backed by the Federal Housing Administration. FHA loans allow as little as 3.5% down and accept credit scores starting in the 580s, making them accessible for first-time and credit-challenged buyers. The tradeoff is a mandatory mortgage insurance premium (MIP), both upfront and annual, which adds to the cost of the loan. FHA is available for primary residences only. FHA also allows multi-family purchases (2 to 4 units) when the buyer occupies one unit, making it a common tool for house hacking.
Fixed-Rate Mortgage
A mortgage where the interest rate, and therefore the principal and interest payment, stays the same for the entire loan term. No market fluctuations, no surprises. The 30-year fixed is the most common structure in the U.S. 15-year and 20-year fixed terms are also widely available and carry lower rates because the lender's exposure period is shorter. Fixed rates are available through conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, and non-QM programs.
Foreign National Loan
A mortgage designed for non-U.S. citizens and non-permanent residents purchasing property in the United States. Foreign national programs are non-QM products that typically require a larger down payment (30% to 40%), a valid passport or visa, and documentation of income or assets from the borrower's home country. They're commonly used by international buyers purchasing vacation homes, investment properties, or relocating to the U.S. for work.
Forgivable Loan
A form of down payment assistance structured as a second mortgage that is gradually forgiven, or canceled outright, over a set period, provided you stay in the home and meet the program's conditions. New York's SONYMA DPAL, for example, offers up to $15,000 that is fully forgiven after a qualifying period. If you sell or refinance before the forgiveness period ends, a prorated portion may become due at closing.

H

HECM (Home Equity Conversion Mortgage)
The FHA-insured reverse mortgage product, and by far the most common type of reverse mortgage in the United States. HECMs allow homeowners 62 and older to access home equity as a lump sum, a line of credit, monthly payments, or a combination, without making monthly mortgage payments. The loan is non-recourse: neither the borrower nor their heirs can owe more than the home is worth at repayment. A HUD-approved counseling session is required before any HECM can proceed.
High-Balance Loan
A conventional mortgage that exceeds the standard conforming loan limit but falls within a higher limit set for designated high-cost areas. In 2025, high-balance limits reach up to $1,209,750 in markets like parts of New York, New Jersey, and California. These loans follow Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines but carry slightly higher rates than standard conforming loans. They're still far more accessible than jumbo loans in terms of documentation and down payment requirements.
House Hacking
A strategy where a buyer purchases a 2-to-4 unit property, lives in one unit as their primary residence, and rents the remaining units to offset the mortgage. Because the buyer occupies the property, FHA financing applies with as little as 3.5% down, a rare case where investment income supports a low-down-payment purchase. The rental income from the other units reduces or eliminates the effective housing cost. After the minimum occupancy period, many buyers move out and convert the property to a full rental.

I

Interest-Only Loan
A mortgage in which the monthly payment during the initial period covers only the accruing interest, not the principal balance. Payments are lower during this phase; once it ends, the loan recasts and principal repayments begin. Real estate investors use interest-only loans to maximize cash flow during a hold period or while executing a value-add strategy. Available as a non-QM product for borrowers who don't qualify under conventional guidelines.
ITIN Loan
A mortgage for borrowers who have an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) but not a Social Security number. ITIN loans allow non-citizens who are ineligible for a Social Security number to purchase property in the U.S. These are non-QM products and typically require a down payment of 20% or more, along with alternative income documentation. They're an important option for immigrant families building roots in the U.S. without the documentation conventional lenders require.

J

Jumbo Loan
A mortgage that exceeds the high-balance conforming loan limits, typically above $1,209,750 in most markets in 2025. Jumbo loans are not sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, so lenders set their own standards. They generally require stronger credit, larger reserves, and bigger down payments than conforming loans. Non-QM jumbo programs exist for self-employed and investor borrowers who have the financial strength to support a large loan but don't fit conventional documentation requirements.

L

Letter of Explanation (LOE)
A brief written statement from the borrower explaining an irregularity in their financial picture: a gap in employment, a large one-time deposit, a credit event, or a seasonal dip in income. LOEs are common in non-QM files where income is complex or variable. A clear, factual explanation (sometimes supported by a note from the borrower's CPA) can prevent a denial on what is essentially a documentation question rather than a genuine underwriting issue.
LLC Vesting
Taking title to a property in the name of a limited liability company (LLC) rather than as an individual. Real estate investors use LLCs to separate personal liability from investment assets and to organize multi-property portfolios by entity. Cliffco's DSCR program allows loans to close in an LLC name, which is a significant advantage, since most conventional investment loans require individual vesting.
Loan-to-Value Ratio (LTV)
The loan amount expressed as a percentage of the property's appraised value. A $400,000 loan on a $500,000 home is an 80% LTV. Higher LTV means more risk for the lender, which is why lower LTVs typically unlock better rates and fewer restrictions. Most non-QM programs allow up to 80% to 90% LTV on primary residences and 75% to 80% on investment properties. A lower LTV can also compensate for a lower credit score or DSCR when the file has other strengths.

N

Non-QM Loan
Any mortgage that falls outside the CFPB's definition of a qualified mortgage. Non-QM is not a synonym for subprime or high-risk. It simply means the loan uses alternative income documentation rather than W-2s and tax returns. Bank statement loans, DSCR loans, P&L loans, 1099 loans, asset depletion, and foreign national loans are all types of non-QM. They exist because the qualified mortgage framework was built around W-2 employees with predictable income, and a large share of creditworthy borrowers don't fit that template.
Non-Warrantable Condo
A condominium that doesn't meet Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac's eligibility requirements for conventional financing. Common disqualifiers include a high percentage of investor-owned units, pending litigation involving the HOA, excessive commercial space in the building, or a budget with inadequate reserves. Non-warrantable condos can still be financed through non-QM programs, typically at slightly higher rates than warrantable properties.

P

P&L Loan (Profit and Loss)
A non-QM mortgage that qualifies self-employed borrowers using a CPA-prepared profit and loss statement rather than tax returns or bank statements. The P&L summarizes business revenue, expenses, and net income over 12 or 24 months. Some programs accept the P&L as the sole income document without bank statement backup. This works best for structured businesses with organized financials and a CPA familiar with what lenders need to see.
PITIA
An acronym for Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance, and any Association (HOA) dues. PITIA represents the total monthly housing cost and is used by lenders to calculate DTI and DSCR. On a DSCR loan, the gross monthly rent is measured against the full PITIA, not just principal and interest. A common oversight for new investors is underestimating taxes, insurance, and HOA when projecting DSCR.
Pre-Approval
A conditional commitment from a lender stating how much you're eligible to borrow, based on verified income, assets, credit, and employment documentation. Pre-approval carries real weight. Sellers take pre-approved buyers more seriously than those with only a pre-qualification. For non-QM borrowers, the pre-approval process also establishes which income documentation method qualifies and at what loan amount, which is important to know before entering a purchase contract.
Pre-Qualification
An early, informal estimate of how much you might be able to borrow, based on self-reported financial information with nothing verified. Pre-qualification is useful for understanding your approximate budget before you start the home search seriously, but it doesn't carry the same weight as a pre-approval when you're ready to make an offer.
Primary Residence
The home where you live the majority of the year. Primary residences qualify for the most favorable rates and underwriting standards, including low-down-payment programs, first-time buyer assistance, and reverse mortgages. Lenders verify occupancy through factors like driver's license address, utility accounts, and employment proximity. Claiming a property as a primary residence when it's intended as an investment is mortgage fraud and carries serious legal consequences.

Q

Qualified Mortgage (QM)
A category of mortgage defined by the CFPB that meets specific underwriting standards: a DTI at or below 43%, no risky features like balloon payments, and income verified through traditional documentation like W-2s and tax returns. Qualified mortgages give lenders a legal safe harbor. Loans outside these standards are non-QM, not because they're dangerous, but because they don't fit the federal template designed around standard employment income.

R

Rate-and-Term Refinance
A refinance in which you replace your existing mortgage with a new one to change the interest rate, the loan term, or both, without pulling cash out. The goal is usually to lower the monthly payment, shorten the payoff timeline, or move from an adjustable rate to a fixed one. Available through conventional, FHA, VA, and non-QM programs. Self-employed borrowers who originally bought with a conventional loan sometimes refinance into a non-QM product after their tax-return situation changes.
Reserves
Cash or liquid assets remaining after your down payment and closing costs are paid. Lenders require reserves as evidence you can continue making payments if income is disrupted. Reserve requirements are higher for investment properties and non-QM loans, typically 3 to 12 months of PITIA in verifiable accounts. Retirement accounts, investment portfolios, and savings all count toward reserves, though retirement account balances are sometimes discounted by lenders when calculating eligible reserves.
Reverse Mortgage
A home loan for borrowers 62 and older that converts home equity into cash without requiring a monthly mortgage payment. Instead of the borrower paying the lender, the lender pays the borrower: as a lump sum, a line of credit, monthly income, or a combination. The loan becomes due when the last borrower sells the home, permanently moves out, or passes away. Most reverse mortgages are FHA-backed HECMs, which are non-recourse, meaning heirs are never responsible for more than the home is worth.

S

Self-Employed Borrower
A borrower who owns a business, works as a freelancer, or earns income outside of traditional W-2 employment. Self-employed borrowers often show lower taxable income than their actual cash flow because of legitimate business deductions, creating a gap between what they earn and what their tax returns reflect. Non-QM programs (bank statement loans, P&L loans, 1099 loans, asset depletion) are specifically designed to qualify these borrowers based on real cash flow rather than taxable income.
Short-Term Rental (STR)
A property rented on a nightly or weekly basis through platforms like Airbnb or VRBO. STRs can generate significantly more income than long-term rentals in the right markets, but they require active management and are subject to local regulations that vary widely. Cliffco's DSCR program accepts STR income in eligible markets and can use market projection data for new purchases without an operating history, including properties in Disney-area Polk County, Florida.
SONYMA
The State of New York Mortgage Agency. SONYMA administers several mortgage assistance programs for income-eligible first-time homebuyers in New York, including the Down Payment Assistance Loan (DPAL), which offers up to $15,000 in forgivable second mortgage assistance. Cliffco is an approved SONYMA lender. SONYMA programs are funded and can run out mid-year, so applying early matters.

U

Underwriting
The process by which a lender evaluates the risk of a mortgage application by reviewing the borrower's income, credit, employment, assets, and the property's appraised value. In conventional lending, underwriting is largely automated (run through Fannie Mae's DU or Freddie Mac's LP). In non-QM lending, underwriting is manual and judgment-based: the underwriter is evaluating a full financial picture, not just running a number through an algorithm. Cliffco underwrites all files in-house.

V

VA Loan
A mortgage guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, available to eligible service members, veterans, and surviving spouses. VA loans offer 0% down, no monthly mortgage insurance, and competitive rates. The VA funding fee (a one-time charge) replaces the mortgage insurance costs that conventional and FHA borrowers pay. VA loans are for primary residences only and are one of the strongest financing tools available for eligible borrowers.
VOE Loan (Verification of Employment Only)
A non-QM mortgage that qualifies W-2 employees based solely on employer verification of income, with no tax returns or pay stubs required. The lender contacts the employer directly to confirm employment status and salary. This works for employees whose returns are complicated by side income, rental property deductions, or other factors that make the return a poor reflection of their actual earning stability.

#

1099 Loan
A non-QM mortgage that qualifies borrowers based on 1099 income forms rather than tax returns. Designed for independent contractors, consultants, gig workers, and freelancers who receive consistent 1099 income without a formal business structure. Lenders typically look at 12 to 24 months of 1099s to establish average annual income. This differs from a bank statement loan in that it relies on documented gross income from the 1099 forms themselves rather than deposit patterns.
