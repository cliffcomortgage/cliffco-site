Who qualifies

This program was built for you specifically.

Conventional mortgage guidelines were written for W-2 employees. If you don't fit that mold, bank statement qualifying changes the equation entirely.

  • ✓ Sole proprietors and single-member LLC owners
  • ✓ S-Corp and C-Corp owners
  • ✓ 1099 contractors and freelancers
  • ✓ Seasonal business owners
  • ✓ Restaurant, retail, and service business owners
  • ✓ Real estate agents and commissioned salespeople
  • ✓ Borrowers whose tax returns show less income than they actually earn

Frequently asked

Bank statement mortgage questions, answered.

What is a bank statement mortgage?

A bank statement mortgage lets you qualify using 12 to 24 months of bank deposits instead of tax returns or W-2s. Lenders analyze your average monthly deposits to calculate qualifying income. This program was built specifically for self-employed borrowers whose tax returns understate their actual cash flow.

How many months of bank statements do I need?

Most programs require 12 months, though some allow 24 months, which can be beneficial if your income has grown over time. Cliffco works with both personal and business bank statements.

What if my business income varies month to month?

Lenders average your monthly deposits over the full statement period. Some variation is expected and accepted. What matters is your consistent average, not any single month's performance.

Can I qualify if my tax returns show a loss or very low income?

Yes, in many cases. Bank statement programs look at actual deposits, not taxable income. If your business generates strong cash flow but your accountant has minimized taxable income through deductions, you may still qualify.

What credit score do I need for a bank statement loan?

Cliffco's bank statement programs typically require a minimum 620 FICO, though some scenarios can work with scores as low as 580 with compensating factors such as a larger down payment or strong reserves.

How is my income calculated from bank statements?

For personal bank statements, lenders typically use 100% of average monthly deposits. For business bank statements, lenders apply an expense factor (typically 50-75%) to account for operating costs. Your loan officer will calculate both and use whichever is more favorable.
