Refinancing

Rate-and-term, cash-out, CEMA, and non-QM refinances for all borrower types.

Cliffco refinances mortgages across all loan types: conventional rate-and-term, cash-out up to 80-85% LTV, non-QM refinances for self-employed borrowers who can't qualify conventionally, CEMA refinances that eliminate New York mortgage recording tax on the existing principal balance, and HECM-to-HECM reverse mortgage refinances for better terms.

Refinance types

Five refinance programs Cliffco originates.

Rate-and-term refinance

Lower your interest rate, shorten or extend your loan term, or switch from an adjustable-rate to a fixed-rate mortgage. The most common refinance type; available on conventional, FHA, VA, and non-QM loans.

Cash-out refinance

Replace your existing mortgage with a larger one and receive the difference in cash. Common uses: home improvements, debt consolidation, down payment on an investment property, or funding a business. Available on conventional (up to 80% LTV), VA (up to 90%), and non-QM programs (up to 75–80%).

CEMA refinance (New York)

A Consolidation, Extension, and Modification Agreement saves New York borrowers mortgage recording tax (up to 1.8% in NYC, 1% elsewhere) by modifying the existing mortgage rather than recording a new one. Cliffco originates CEMAs statewide and is one of a shrinking number of lenders that still does them efficiently.

Non-QM refinance

For self-employed borrowers, 1099 contractors, and anyone who can't document income conventionally. Cliffco refinances using bank statements, P&L, 1099s, or asset depletion, on both rate-and-term and cash-out transactions.

HECM-to-HECM reverse mortgage refinance

Existing reverse mortgage holders can refinance into a new HECM to access a higher line of credit (if home values have risen), add a younger spouse to the loan, or secure better terms. Cliffco's reverse mortgage team handles HECM-to-HECM refinances in all states where we're licensed.

When to refinance

The break-even math.

A refinance costs money upfront (closing costs typically 2–3% of the loan balance). The question is whether the monthly savings justify that cost before you sell or pay off the home.

Break-even example

Closing costs
$8,000
Monthly savings
$250
Break-even point
32 months (~2.7 years)

If you plan to stay in the home past 32 months, the refinance saves money. If not, it may not be worth the closing costs.

Frequently asked

Refinance questions, answered.

When does refinancing make sense?

Refinancing typically makes sense when you can lower your rate by at least 0.5–1 point, reduce your term, switch from an ARM to a fixed rate, or access equity. The break-even calculation: divide your closing costs by the monthly savings to find how many months to recoup the cost. If you plan to stay past that point, a refi usually pencils out.

What is a CEMA refinance and who can use it?

A Consolidation, Extension, and Modification Agreement (CEMA) is a New York-specific refinance structure that modifies your existing mortgage rather than paying it off and recording a new one. Because mortgage recording tax (up to 1.8% in NYC, 1% elsewhere in NY) only applies to new principal, a CEMA can save thousands. It requires lender cooperation and takes longer than a standard refi but is usually worth it for balances above $200K.

Can I do a cash-out refinance if I'm self-employed?

Yes. If you can't qualify for a conventional cash-out refi using tax returns, Cliffco's non-QM programs allow cash-out refinances using bank statements, P&L, 1099s, or asset depletion, up to 75–80% LTV on most programs.

How long does a refinance take?

A conventional refinance typically closes in 21–45 days. Non-QM and CEMA refinances can take 30–60 days. Florida refinances involving title and insurance require additional lead time. Your Cliffco LO will give you a realistic timeline at the application stage.

What's the maximum LTV for a cash-out refinance?

Conventional (Fannie/Freddie): up to 80% LTV on primary residences. FHA: up to 80% LTV. VA: up to 90% LTV for eligible veterans. Non-QM: typically 75–80% LTV, with some programs reaching 85% for strong borrower profiles. Investment properties generally max out at 75% LTV.

Can I refinance a reverse mortgage?

Yes. HECM-to-HECM refinances allow existing reverse mortgage holders to refinance into a new HECM for better terms, a higher line of credit (if home values have risen), or to add a spouse to the loan. Cliffco's reverse mortgage team handles HECM-to-HECM refinances in all states where we're licensed.
